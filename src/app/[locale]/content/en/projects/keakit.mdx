KeaKit is a web app that brings item rentals and service hiring together in one place, letting users build personalized "kits" around what they actually need. It works both ways: people looking for something find it quickly, while owners of unused items can turn them into extra income, all through a centralized and hassle-free process.

## My role

I collaborated within a multidisciplinary team of more than 20 people, working under Scrum and agile practices. My contributions included market research and analysis of competing platforms, plus ongoing development of the platform: implementing new features, refactoring existing components and fixing bugs.

## Features

- **User management**: registration, login, profile editing and a rating system.
- **Item listings**: upload, edit and manage items available for rent.
- **Custom kits**: flexible combination of products and services into a single package.
- **Dynamic pricing**: real-time cost based on the kit configuration.
- **Logistics**: selecting dates, locations and delivery or pickup conditions.
- **Integrated payments**: secure hiring and payment directly through the platform.
- **Rental tracking**: monitoring the status of active orders.

## Technical challenges

- Coordinating within a large multidisciplinary team under Scrum, keeping development aligned with the rest of the team.
- Conducting market research and analyzing competing platforms.
- Implementing new features and refactoring existing components without breaking functionality already in use across the platform.
- Diagnosing and fixing bugs in a codebase built by many contributors, which meant understanding logic and structures I hadn't written.

## Takeaways

- Contributing to a real-world-like platform within a team of more than 20 people, under Scrum and agile practices.
- Market research and competitor analysis directly shape product decisions.
- Working on an evolving codebase built my skills in implementing features, refactoring and fixing bugs while keeping the platform stable for users and other developers.
